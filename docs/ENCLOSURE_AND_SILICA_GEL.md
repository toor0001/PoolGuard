# Enclosure moisture protection and silica gel

PoolGuard is installed only a few centimetres above the pool-water surface. The electronics therefore have to cope with high relative humidity, temperature changes and possible condensation even when they are protected from direct splashes.

## Do not pot the complete enclosure

Do not fill the complete electronics compartment with epoxy and do not simply coat the whole enclosure in silicone. Full potting makes later repair difficult, can trap moisture that is already inside and can create mechanical stress around connectors and cables. Silicone is useful selectively at cable entries, seams and other known splash paths, but it should not be used as a substitute for a deliberate enclosure design.

The existing openings around the skimmer-lid ribs mean that the compartment is not hermetically sealed. This is not automatically a problem: preventing liquid water and splashes from reaching the PCB is more important than trying to make a small DIY enclosure perfectly airtight. Any opening should be arranged so that water cannot run directly onto the electronics and so that accumulated water cannot remain trapped inside.

## Silica gel

A small **silica-gel desiccant sachet** inside the electronics compartment is a useful additional precaution. Use a proper porous sachet; do not pour loose beads into the enclosure and do not use table salt. The sachet should be fixed so it cannot touch moving parts, connectors or exposed contacts and cannot fall into the pool.

Silica gel is **not permanent**. It adsorbs water from the enclosed air until it approaches saturation. How long that takes cannot be specified as a fixed number of days because it depends strongly on:

- the amount of silica gel;
- how open the enclosure is to ambient air;
- pool-water and air temperature;
- daily temperature swings and condensation;
- how often the enclosure is opened.

In a nearly sealed small box it can remain useful for months. In a ventilated box above warm pool water it can saturate much faster because new humid air continuously enters. For PoolGuard it should therefore be regarded as a **replaceable/regenerable moisture buffer**, not as the primary waterproofing method.

Indicator silica gel is especially convenient because its colour shows when regeneration is required. Follow the manufacturer's regeneration temperature and time; many reusable silica-gel products can be dried and reused repeatedly. Do not assume one universal oven temperature, because sachet materials and indicator chemistry differ.

## Practical recommendation for PoolGuard

1. Keep direct rain, splashes and dripping water away from the electronics.
2. Seal obvious cable-entry and seam leak paths selectively where useful.
3. Do not completely encapsulate the assembled electronics in silicone or epoxy.
4. Add a small replaceable silica-gel sachet as a secondary moisture buffer.
5. Inspect the enclosure occasionally for condensation, corrosion or a saturated indicator sachet, especially after large day/night temperature changes.
6. Replace or regenerate the silica gel when its indicator shows saturation or when moisture is found inside.

Silica gel reduces humidity peaks but cannot compensate for an enclosure into which liquid water can enter.
